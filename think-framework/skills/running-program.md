# 5K Running Program

## Purpose

Use this guide to build a gradual 5K plan, especially for a runner targeting a sub-30-minute finish.

## Training principles

- A 30-minute 5K requires an average pace of approximately 6:00 per kilometer.
- Most running should feel easy enough to speak in short sentences.
- Increase weekly training gradually rather than changing distance and intensity at the same time.
- Include at least one easy or rest day after a demanding session.
- Warm up before faster running and cool down afterward.

## Example training week

### Session 1: Easy run

- Run or run-walk for 25–40 minutes at a conversational effort.
- The goal is aerobic fitness, not speed.

### Session 2: Intervals

- Warm up with 10 minutes of easy jogging and dynamic movement.
- Complete 4–6 repetitions of 400 meters at approximately target 5K pace.
- Recover with 200 meters of walking or easy jogging between repetitions.
- Cool down for 5–10 minutes.

### Session 3: Recovery or cross-training

- Choose walking, cycling, swimming, or light strength work for 20–40 minutes.
- Keep the effort easy.

### Session 4: Longer easy run

- Run or run-walk for 35–55 minutes at a comfortable effort.
- Increase duration only when the previous week felt manageable.

## Progression

- Add no more than about 5–10 percent to total weekly running when recovery is good.
- Every third or fourth week, maintain or slightly reduce volume.
- After several consistent weeks, extend intervals or shorten recovery rather than immediately running faster.

## Knee considerations

- Prefer flat, predictable surfaces while symptoms are present.
- Shorten the run or use run-walk intervals if discomfort increases.
- Avoid increasing speed, hills, and weekly distance simultaneously.
- Stop if pain becomes sharp, changes running mechanics, or continues to worsen.
- Swelling, locking, instability, or persistent pain warrants evaluation by a qualified healthcare professional.

## Readiness checks

Before progressing, confirm that:

- Pain did not increase during the previous session.
- Symptoms returned to their usual level by the following day.
- Sleep and general fatigue are manageable.
- Running form remains controlled near the end of the session.
