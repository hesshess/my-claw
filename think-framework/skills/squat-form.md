# Squat Form Guide

## Purpose

Use this guide when explaining squat technique, correcting form, or adapting squats for knee discomfort.

## Setup

- Place the feet approximately shoulder-width apart.
- Turn the toes slightly outward, usually 5–20 degrees.
- Keep the whole foot in contact with the floor: heel, base of the big toe, and base of the little toe.
- Brace the abdomen as if preparing for a light punch.
- Keep the chest comfortably tall and the spine neutral.

## Movement

1. Begin by bending the hips and knees together.
2. Let the knees travel in the same direction as the toes.
3. Lower only as far as a neutral spine and stable foot position can be maintained.
4. Keep the bar or body weight balanced over the middle of the foot.
5. Push the floor away and extend the hips and knees together to stand.
6. Finish tall without forcefully leaning backward.

## Breathing and bracing

- Take a breath before descending.
- Brace the trunk throughout the repetition.
- Exhale after passing the hardest part of the ascent, then reset before the next repetition.

## Common mistakes

- Knees collapsing inward.
- Heels or toes lifting from the floor.
- Losing abdominal tension and rounding the lower back.
- Descending deeper than current mobility allows.
- Increasing weight before the movement is controlled.

## If the user has knee discomfort

- Do not recommend training through sharp, increasing, or unstable pain.
- Reduce the load and range of motion first.
- Consider a box squat or assisted squat to control depth.
- Use a slow, controlled descent and keep the knee aligned with the toes.
- Stop the exercise if pain worsens, the knee swells, locks, or gives way.
- Persistent pain should be assessed by an appropriately qualified healthcare professional.

## Coaching checklist

- Stable tripod foot
- Knees tracking with toes
- Braced trunk
- Controlled depth
- Balanced over mid-foot
- Pain-free or tolerable movement
